# Platform defensibility and ecosystem strategy

Status: product strategy baseline

## Principle

NetCityOS should build durable differentiation through protected technology,
operational trust, ecosystem quality, and accumulated customer value. The goal
is a defensible Enterprise standard, not an undocumented trap that prevents a
customer from accessing its own configurations or evidence.

## Defensible layers

1. **KVP technical implementation** — protected command/evidence semantics,
   session binding, idempotent reconciliation, adapter capability model.
2. **Architect OS semantic graph** — versioned cross-provider architecture model,
   compiler, simulation, drift, and evidence linkage.
3. **Enterprise policy and evidence** — reusable governed scenarios, control
   packs, approvals, evaluations, and operational history.
4. **Connector and conformance ecosystem** — SDK, compatibility suite, signed
   releases, and `KVP Ready` program after trademark/certification review.
5. **Operational excellence** — migration tooling, predictable upgrades,
   incident controls, support, and verified deployment patterns.

## Lawful switching value

Customer retention should come from the continuing value of its architecture
graph, policies, evidence, evaluations, and integrations. NetCityOS will define
versioned export formats for customer-owned configuration and evidence while
retaining proprietary implementation, certification tests, optimizers, and
non-public know-how. Interoperability improves standard adoption and reduces
procurement resistance in the Enterprise segment.

## Ecosystem control points

- canonical KVP compatibility profile and conformance test authority;
- signed adapter/provider capability registry;
- versioned architecture and policy schemas;
- Enterprise certification, support, and long-term compatibility commitments;
- trademark-controlled compatibility marks;
- commercial SDK/runtime licensing with a documented open interoperability edge.

## Claims discipline

Commercial material must not say “unhackable,” “cryptographically protects a
cloud provider's internals,” or “proves KV-cache integrity” without matching
implemented evidence. Precise, verifiable claims are more credible to regulated
customers and safer for patent, security, and procurement review.
